# Institutions attributed to popes in Continua

Explicit acts the app's Matins lessons and martyrology entries say a pope instituted, ordained, decreed, or established. Latin first, then English. Reign dates are from the Wikipedia list of popes; early dates are approximate. Popes with no such act in the text are omitted. Quotations are copied verbatim from the source files. Where an English version is marked "(translation mine)", the app has no English for that passage. Entries whose source line says "reference engine; not yet in the app" come from the Rome-city calendar files (Sancti/Urbis), which have no English, so the English there is my translation. Telesphorus and Hyginus come from the reference engine's Rome-calendar files and are now in the app as their Matins lessons; the Latin below is quoted from the reference with its typos, and the app files carry the corrected text.

## St. Linus

Reign: c. 64–c. 76 (dates uncertain).

- **Women must veil their heads in church.**
  - Latin: Sancívit ne qua mulier, nisi velato cápite, in ecclésiam introíret.
  - English: He forbade women to enter the Church without having a veil upon their heads.
  - Source: src/data/propers/nocturn-readings/st-linus.yml

## Sts. Cletus and Anacletus

Reign: c. 76–c. 88 (dates uncertain).

- **First pope to use the salutation "Health and Apostolic Benediction" in his letters.**
  - Latin: Primus in litteris verbis illis usus est: Salutem et apostolicam benedictionem.
  - English: He was the first Pope who made use in his letters of the phrase "Health and Apostolic Benediction."
  - Source: src/data/propers/nocturn-readings/ss-cletus-and-marcellinus.yml

- **Consecration of a bishop by three bishops; public ordination by one's own bishop; all to Communicate at Mass; a burial place for popes (St. Anacletus lesson).**
  - Latin: Decrevit, ut episcopus a tribus episcopis, neque a paucioribus consecraretur, et clerici sacris ordinibus publice a proprio episcopo initiarentur: et ut in Missa, peracta consecratione omnes communicarent. Beati Petri sepulcrum ornavit, Pontificumque sepulturæ locum attribuit.
  - English: He ordained that a Bishop should be consecrated by three Bishops and no less, that clerks should be publicly ordained to Holy Orders by their own Bishop, and that in the Mass, after the Consecration, all should afterwards Communicate. He adorned the grave of Blessed Peter, and ordered a place for burying the Popes in.
  - Source: src/data/propers/nocturn-readings/st-anacletus.yml

  - English: He it was who divided the seven quarters of the city among seven scribes, one to each, whose duty it was to search out most carefully, and record in writing the sufferings and acts of the Martyrs.

## St. Alexander I

Reign: c. 105–c. 115 (dates uncertain).

- **Bread and wine alone offered at the Mass, water mixed with the wine.**
  - Latin: Is constítuit ut tantummodo panis et vinum in mysterio offerrétur: vinum autem aqua miscéri jussit, propter sánguinem et aquam, quæ ex Jesu Christi látere profluxérunt;
  - English: He ordained that nothing but bread and wine should be offered at the mystery, but that some water should be mingled with the wine, in memory of the Blood and Water Which flowed from the Side of Jesus Christ.

  - Latin: et in Cánone Missæ addidit, Qui pridie quam paterétur.
  - English: He added to the Canon of the Mass the words Who, the night before He suffered.
  - Source: src/data/propers/nocturn-readings/ss-alexander-eventius-theodulus-and-juvenal.yml

- **Holy water mingled with salt kept in churches and used in homes.**
  - Latin: Idem decrevit ut aqua benedicta, sale admixto, perpetuo in ecclésia asservarétur, et in cubiculis adhiberétur ad fugándos dæmones.

## St. Sixtus I

Reign: c. 115–c. 125 (approximate).

- **Sacred vessels not to be handled by non-ministers; the corporal to be linen; the Trisagion sung by the people at Mass; bishops summoned to Rome to carry apostolic letters back to their dioceses.**
  - Latin: Nonnulla ad disciplínam ecclesiásticam pertinéntia prudentíssime státuit, scílicet, ne sacra vasa et ministeria ab áliis, qui sacri minístri non essent , contrectarentur : ne sacrum corporale, ut vocant, ex ália, quam linea matéria fíeret: ut sacrum Trisagium in Missa pópulus decantaret : et ne ullus Epíscopus Romam evocátus ad suam deínde diœcesim rédiens susciperetur, nisi lítteris apostólicis, quas Formatas et Canonicas antiqui vocabant, munítus esset.
  - English: He prudently established certain things pertaining to ecclesiastical discipline, namely: that the sacred vessels and furnishings not be handled by others who were not sacred ministers; that the sacred corporal, as they call it, not be made of any material other than linen; that the people sing the holy Trisagion at Mass; and that no bishop summoned to Rome be received on returning to his diocese unless he were provided with apostolic letters, which the ancients called Formatae and Canonical. (translation mine)
  - Source: ../divinum-officium-reference/web/www/horas/Latin/Sancti/Urbis/04-06-XystusI.txt (reference engine; not yet in the app)

## St. Hyginus

Reign: c. 136–c. 140 (approximate).

- **Distinguished the clerical orders by grade; principal and subordinate ministers in each titular church.**
  - Latin: clericórum ordines in suos gradus distínxit, ac fecit, ut in iis ecclésiis, quas Títulos vocant, tum primarii minístri, tum álii eísdem subjecti adessent, qui fidélibus, cum res postularet, sacra ministrarent.
  - English: he distinguished the orders of clerics into their grades, and arranged that in those churches called Titles there should be both principal ministers and others subject to them, to minister the sacred things to the faithful when need required. (translation mine)
  - Source: src/data/propers/nocturn-readings/st-hyginus.yml (from the reference engine's Sancti/Urbis/01-11cc.txt; Latin typos corrected there)

- **No consecration of churches without Mass; none built or destroyed without the bishop; church materials not put to profane use.**
  - Latin: Instítuit étiam ne absque celebratióne Missæ templa vel oratoria sacro ritu consecrarentur: ne inconsulto epíscopo, vel nova construerentur, vel jam constructa destruerentur, et ne materies ad sacrum illum usum congesta et destinata, in profanos usus converteretur.
  - English: He also established that temples or oratories not be consecrated with sacred rite without the celebration of Mass; that new ones not be built, nor existing ones destroyed, without the bishop being consulted; and that the material gathered and set apart for that sacred use not be turned to profane uses. (translation mine)
  - Source: src/data/propers/nocturn-readings/st-hyginus.yml (from the reference engine's Sancti/Urbis/01-11cc.txt; Latin typos corrected there)

- **Godparents at baptism.**
  - Latin: Edixit prætérea, ut patrini baptísmo adessent, qui de sacro fonte infantes levarent:
  - English: He further decreed that godparents be present at baptism to lift the infants from the sacred font; (translation mine)
  - Source: src/data/propers/nocturn-readings/st-hyginus.yml (from the reference engine's Sancti/Urbis/01-11cc.txt; Latin typos corrected there)

- **No bishop to be condemned unless tried by the bishops of his province.**
  - Latin: ne quis epíscopus reus críminis damnaretur, nisi a comprovincialibus epíscopis ejus causa probe discussa fuísset.
  - English: and that no bishop be condemned as guilty of a crime unless his case had been properly examined by the bishops of his province. (translation mine)

## St. Lucius I

Reign: 25 June 253–5 March 254.

- **Two priests and three deacons always to attend the bishop as witnesses; peace to be given to the lapsed after penance.**
  - Latin: Sanxit, ut duo presbyteri ac tres diáconi, ecclesiástici causa testimonii, quovis loco epíscopum non desererent Pacem lapsis dandam, et pœniténtia acta, communicationis fructum negandum non esse, spíritu Dei plenus (ut sanctus Cypriánus scripsit), lítteris suis significávit.
  - English: He ordained that two priests and three deacons, for the sake of ecclesiastical testimony, should not abandon the bishop in any place. And, full of the Spirit of God (as St. Cyprian wrote), he made known by his letters that peace must be given to the lapsed, and that, once penance has been done, the fruit of communion is not to be denied. ## St. Silvester I

Reign: 314–335.

- **Chrism to be made only by a bishop; priests to anoint the newly baptized; deacons' dalmatic and linen maniple; the altar sacrifice only on a linen cloth.**

## St. Pius X

Reign: 1903–1914.

- **Restored sacred music; Rome the centre of biblical study; reformed the Curia; catechism laws; frequent and daily Communion, and early children's Communion; one code of Church law; ended the civil veto in papal elections.**
  - Latin: Vir acérrimi ingénii et propósiti tenax, inter vicésimi ineúntis sæculi procéllas, Ecclésiam fírmiter rexit, et præclaríssimis ornávit institútis. Músicam sacram ad prístinum splendórem ac dignitátem revocávit; sacrórum Bibliórum stúdiis príncipem sedem Romæ constítuit; Románam Cúriam sapiénter reformávit; leges de fidélibus per catechísmum instituéndis restítuit; Eucharísticæ mensæ crebriórem, imo et cotidiánam consuetúdinem indúxit, ejúsque accéssum púeris quoque a primo ratiónis usu apéruit; actiónis cathólicæ increménta sédulo promóvit; sólidæ cleri institutióni provídit, ádditis quoque semináriis per regiónes dispósitis; sacerdótes omnes ad interiórem vitam coléndam alléxit; leges Ecclésiæ in unum corpus redégit; erróres perniciosíssimos, modernísmi appellatióne comprehénsos, damnávit atque evéllit; civíle vétitum, quod dicunt, in Pontíficis Máximi electióne rejécit.
  - English: A man of passionate temperament and of firm purpose, he ruled the Church firmly as it entered into the twentieth century, and adorned it with brilliant teachings. He restored the sacred music to its pristine glory and dignity; he established Rome as the principal centre for the study of the Holy Bible; he ordered the reform of the Roman Curia with great wisdom; he restored the laws concerning the faithful for the instruction of the catechism; he introduced the custom of more frequent and even daily reception of the Holy Eucharist, as well as permitting its reception by children as soon as they reach the age of reason; he zealously promoted the growth of Catholic action; he provided for the sound education of clerics and increased the number of seminaries in their diverse regions; he encouraged every priest in the practice of the interior life; he brought the laws of the Church together into one body; he condemned and suppressed those most pernicious errors known collectively as Modernism; he suppressed the custom of civil veto at the election of a Supreme Pontiff.

# Feast days and patronages fixed by popes

A second list from the martyrology: popes who set a saint's feast day, ordered a feast, or named a patron. Grouped by pope, in order of reign. Canonizations and doctor declarations are left out.
